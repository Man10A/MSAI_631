Key Topics in MSAI-631

Design thinking:
Design thinking is a human-centered process for solving problems. It is often described
with five stages: empathize, define, ideate, prototype, and test. The goal is to deeply
understand users and iterate toward better solutions.
Source: https://video.alexanderstreet.com/watch/design-thinking-3

Human-centered AI:
Human-centered AI focuses on AI systems that support and augment people rather than
replace them. It emphasizes transparency, control, trust, and alignment with human goals.
Source: complete-course-explanation.docx synthesis

Multimodal interaction:
Multimodal interfaces support more than one mode of input and output, such as touch,
speech, and visuals. In daily life, this can include voice assistants, gesture-based
controls, and systems that combine text, images, and sound.
Source: complete-course-explanation.docx synthesis

Conversational agents and chatbots:
Conversational agents allow users to interact with systems through natural language.
They can be rule-based or powered by large language models. Good chatbots use clear
flows, polite error handling, and simple language, and they often provide a way to
reach a human when needed.
Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/bot-service/bot-service-overview?view=azure-bot-service-4.0

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):
RAG combines information retrieval with language generation. First, the system retrieves
relevant text chunks from a knowledge base, such as course documents. Then an LLM is
used to generate an answer using only that retrieved context. This helps reduce
hallucinations and keeps answers grounded in real documents.
Source: complete-course-explanation.docx synthesis

User-centered design (UCD):
User-centered design means starting from real users’ goals, tasks, and contexts instead
of starting from the technology. Designers use empathy, user research, and iteration to
make systems simple, clear, accessible, and under the user’s control. UCD is the
“practice side” of HCI: it turns research about how people think and work into concrete
interface decisions.
Source: https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/topics/user-centered-design

Human–computer interaction (HCI) and human–AI systems:
HCI studies how people interact with computers and digital systems: how they perceive
screens, make mistakes, feel frustrated, or enjoy interfaces. Modern HCI also covers
human–AI systems, where AI acts as an assistant or partner. The key idea is that
technology should adapt to people, not the other way around.
Source: Chignell et al. (2023) summary via Google Scholar

Usability and UX for public services:
Government UX guidance stresses clear tasks, plain language, mobile-friendly layouts,
and strong accessibility. Designers are encouraged to test with real users, fix problems
based on evidence, and make sure websites work for people with disabilities and
different backgrounds. When AI is added to public services, these same principles still
apply.
Source: https://digital.gov/topics/usability/

AI and HCI interactions and synergies:
AI brings learning, prediction, and automation; HCI brings methods to keep systems
usable, accessible, and fair. Together they create human–AI systems where people stay
in control, can understand what the AI is doing, and can question or override decisions.
Examples include recommendation systems, medical decision-support tools, and AI
assistants that are designed for trust and transparency.
Source: complete-course-explanation.docx synthesis

AI-powered chatbots and conversational agents:
Modern chatbots combine HCI and AI: they use natural language interfaces, often across
channels like web, mobile, and chat apps. Good design for bots means clear intents,
helpful error messages (“Did you mean…?”), an easy way to reach a human, and
protection of privacy. AI models handle understanding and generation, while the HCI
layer shapes the conversation flow and user experience.
Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/bot-service/bot-service-overview?view=azure-bot-service-4.0

Personalization vs customization in AI systems:
Personalization is when the system adapts automatically based on data and predictions;
customization is when users manually change settings and preferences. Both can improve
experience, but they must be transparent and reversible so users understand what is
happening and can stay in control. In AI systems, designers need to explain why content
is recommended and give clear options to change or turn off personalization.
Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/customization-personalization/
