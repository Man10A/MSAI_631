## syllabus.txt
This course examines the convergence of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) and how they revolutionize the way humans interact with machines and digital systems. The course emphasizes the symbiotic relationship between AI and HCI and prepares students to design, develop, and evaluate intelligent interactive systems. COURSE OBJECTIVES Upon completion of this course, students will:

## syllabus.txt
Understand foundational concepts of AI and HCI and how their integration shapes modern interactive systems. Apply user-centered design principles, usability heuristics, and accessibility guidelines. Integrate machine learning techniques such as classification, clustering, and pattern recognition into adaptive interfaces. Develop AI-powered natural language interfaces including conversational agents and chatbots.

## syllabus.txt
Identify ethical challenges including bias, privacy, transparency, and accountability in AI systems. Design, prototype, and evaluate AI-driven interactive systems using usability testing and iterative refinement. LEARNER OUTCOMES Students will be able to: Analyze AI and HCI and their interaction. Analyze multimodal interfaces. Evaluate user-centric design principles. Create and discuss usability heuristics. Evaluate accessibility guidelines and their legal and ethical implications.

Key Topics in MSAI-631 Design thinking: Design thinking is a human-centered process for solving problems. It is often described with five stages: empathize, define, ideate, prototype, and test. The goal is to deeply understand users and iterate toward better solutions. Source: https://video.alexanderstreet.com/watch/design-thinking-3 Human-centered AI: Human-centered AI focuses on AI systems that support and augment people rather than

## topics.txt

Source: complete-course-explanation.docx synthesis Multimodal interaction: Multimodal interfaces support more than one mode of input and output, such as touch, speech, and visuals. In daily life, this can include voice assistants, gesture-based controls, and systems that combine text, images, and sound. Source: complete-course-explanation.docx synthesis Conversational agents and chatbots:

## topics.txt
Conversational agents allow users to interact with systems through natural language. They can be rule-based or powered by large language models. Good chatbots use clear flows, polite error handling, and simple language, and they often provide a way to reach a human when needed. Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/bot-service/bot-service-overview?view=azure-bot-service-4.0 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):

## topics.txt
RAG combines information retrieval with language generation. First, the system retrieves relevant text chunks from a knowledge base, such as course documents. Then an LLM is used to generate an answer using only that retrieved context. This helps reduce hallucinations and keeps answers grounded in real documents. Source: complete-course-explanation.docx synthesis User-centered design (UCD): User-centered design means starting from real users’ goals, tasks, and contexts instead

## topics.txt
of starting from the technology. Designers use empathy, user research, and iteration to make systems simple, clear, accessible, and under the user’s control. UCD is the “practice side” of HCI: it turns research about how people think and work into concrete interface decisions. Source: https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/topics/user-centered-design Human–computer interaction (HCI) and human–AI systems: HCI studies how people interact with computers and digital systems: how they perceive

## topics.txt

The key idea is that technology should adapt to people, not the other way around. Source: Chignell et al. (2023) summary via Google Scholar Usability and UX for public services: Government UX guidance stresses clear tasks, plain language, mobile-friendly layouts, and strong accessibility. Designers are encouraged to test with real users, fix problems

based on evidence, and make sure websites work for people with disabilities and different backgrounds. When AI is added to public services, these same principles still apply. Source: https://digital.gov/topics/usability/ AI and HCI interactions and synergies: AI brings learning, prediction, and automation; HCI brings methods to keep systems usable, accessible, and fair. Together they create human–AI systems where people stay

Examples include recommendation systems, medical decision-support tools, and AI assistants that are designed for trust and transparency. Source: complete-course-explanation.docx synthesis AI-powered chatbots and conversational agents: Modern chatbots combine HCI and AI: they use natural language interfaces, often across channels like web, mobile, and chat apps. AI models handle understanding and generation, while the HCI layer shapes the conversation flow and user experience.
